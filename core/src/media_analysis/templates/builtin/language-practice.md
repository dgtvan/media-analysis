name: Language practice
description: A conversation whose purpose is practising a language (e.g. English speaking practice with a tutor or partner).
signals:
  - a learner speaking a non-native language, often with a tutor or partner
  - corrections, explanations of grammar or vocabulary, repeated phrases
  - topics typical of conversation classes (hobbies, travel, daily life, role plays)
evidence:
  - lesson material, vocabulary lists or exercises shown on screen
sections:
  - Summary
  - Participants and topics
  - Learner assessment
  - Grammar mistakes and corrections
  - Vocabulary
  - Pronunciation and fluency notes
  - Corrections given by the tutor
  - Suggested exercises
---
Write feedback the learner can use to improve. Identify the learner (the non-native speaker being practised with);
if there are several, assess each separately.

- Learner assessment: overall level (rough CEFR estimate with a short justification), strengths and main weaknesses.
- Grammar mistakes and corrections: a table Original (as said) | Corrected | Explanation | Timecode. Only real mistakes
  from the transcript; do not invent any. Note that the transcript itself may contain recognition errors.
- Vocabulary: words/phrases used well, words that were misused, and better alternatives; new vocabulary introduced.
- Pronunciation and fluency notes: only what the transcript supports (hesitations, restarts, filler words, words the
  speech recognition clearly struggled with); say plainly that pronunciation cannot be judged fully from text.
- Corrections given by the tutor: what the tutor corrected or explained.
- Suggested exercises: 3-5 concrete exercises targeting the weaknesses found.
